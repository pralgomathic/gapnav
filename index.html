GapNav Experimental Evaluation.

Overview

Legged robots now move reliably over rough terrain, stairs, rubble, and cluttered environments, but very tight passages remain difficult. The challenge is often not whether the robot can fit at the entrance, but whether it can keep enough side clearance throughout the crossing. Small yaw errors, lateral corrections, or body-height changes can quickly use up the limited remaining space. Standard navigation systems usually check the opening with a fixed body model and leave the rest to the low-level controller, which is often not enough when the clearance is only a few centimeters.

We present GapNav, a passage-level controller for near-clearance traversal in legged robots. The key idea is to treat the remaining side margin as the quantity to estimate before entry and preserve during traversal. GapNav uses a motion-aware used-width model, a conservative entry rule under bounded uncertainty, and a dedicated passage mode that adjusts motion after entry to reduce unnecessary margin loss.

We evaluate GapNav in Isaac Sim across multiple environments with varied passage layouts, and on a physical quadruped in real-world narrow-passage trials. In simulation, GapNav reaches 88.1 ± 1.7% success with 0.74 ± 0.09 cm margin error while improving all main metrics over the baselines. On hardware, it achieves 46.7% success even at 2 cm side margin per side, and performance improves as more clearance becomes available.

GapNav Framework

GapNav is a passage-level navigation framework for legged robots operating near the physical clearance limit. It builds a local passage representation from LiDAR, odometry, IMU, and proprioceptive feedback, then estimates passage width, robot offset, yaw misalignment, and remaining side margin. Instead of using a fixed footprint check, GapNav models the robot’s motion-aware used width under yaw, lateral drift, and body-height changes. A conservative entry check activates passage mode only when the predicted margin exceeds a safety threshold. During traversal, the policy uses the encoded passage map, state estimate, and motion history to generate velocity, yaw-rate, and body-height commands that preserve clearance through the full passage.
